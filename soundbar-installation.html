SOUNDBAR
MOUNTED RIGHT

A soundbar balanced on a shelf under a wall-mounted TV always looks like an afterthought, because it is one. Mounted properly it sits square, centred and floating with the screen — and nothing rattles when the bass hits.

Why mounting it beats standing it up

Most soundbars ship with a little stand, and most people use it because it's there. It works, but it costs you three things.

It fills your shelf. A 40-inch bar takes the whole surface, so the console below the TV stops being useful for anything else.

It's rarely centred. Sitting on furniture, a bar lines up with the cabinet rather than the screen. Once you notice it's off-centre you can't stop noticing.

It buzzes. A bar resting on a hollow media unit turns it into a resonating box. Mounted to the wall, that stops entirely.

Mounted, it also sits at ear height rather than firing across a coffee table, which is what it was designed for.

How we set it up

The bar goes directly below the TV, centred to the screen rather than the furniture, with a consistent gap. If your TV is on a full-motion mount we account for the swing so the bar never fouls the arm.

Cables get run the same way as the TV's — visible and tidy, in an external strip, or inside the wall if you're already having that done. A soundbar adds a power lead and usually one HDMI, and those are the two that end up dangling if nobody plans for them.
