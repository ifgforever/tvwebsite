Why the wall matters more than the TV

Almost every “near me” search ends with someone worrying about the size of their television. In practice the TV is rarely the problem — an 85″ on timber studs is a straightforward job. What actually decides how a mount goes in is what's behind the drywall.

Chicago housing stock makes this genuinely varied. A 1920s two-flat on the Northwest Side is often plaster over lath, which hides the studs from a magnet and punishes guesswork. Downtown high-rises and a lot of newer construction use metal studs, which need different anchors and won't hold a lag bolt the way wood does. Older brick and concrete need masonry anchors and a hammer drill, not a driver.

This is the part that separates a mount that's solid in ten years from one that sags. It's also why we ask about the wall on the phone — it tells us far more than the screen size does.
