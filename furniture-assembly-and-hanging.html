Shelves, mirrors and gallery walls

Anything heavy on a wall is the same problem as a TV: what's behind the plaster, and whether the anchor matches it. A floating shelf is worse than a TV in one respect — the load sits out from the wall on a lever, so a bracket that would hold a flat-mounted screen can still tear out.

Gallery walls are the ones people underestimate. Hanging eight frames so they read as a set takes measuring and levelling, not eight nails. That's usually an hour or two rather than the twenty minutes people picture.

Mirrors are the ones worth not guessing on. A large mirror is heavy, brittle and unforgiving, and it should be anchored into framing rather than hoping a drywall plug holds.

Flat-pack assembly

Media consoles, bookcases, desks, bed frames, wardrobes. Assembled square, solid and set exactly where you want it — which matters more than it sounds, because a bookcase built in the wrong corner of the room is a two-person problem afterwards.

Most single items land inside an hour. A wardrobe or a bed frame usually runs longer, and anything with doors needs alignment time at the end or it never closes properly.

If you're having a TV mounted anyway, adding the console it sits above is the efficient version of this — one visit, one trip, and we can line the two up together.
